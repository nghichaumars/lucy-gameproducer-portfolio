My approach

Know what the game should feel like, then ship it on time with the team.

Great games come from a clear player fantasy, fast prototypes, honest playtests, and a team that knows exactly what to build next.

  1. 01

    Find the Fun

    Turn player needs and market research into a clear vision, audience and priorities.

  2. 02

    Prototype Fast

    Build AI-assisted playable prototypes in 3–5 days to test the idea early.

  3. 03

    Plan the Build

    Set scope and milestones, run weekly sprints in Jira, and keep the GDD current.

  4. 04

    Test with Players

    Run concept, gameplay, core loop and soft-launch tests, then act on results.

  5. 05

    Ship & Iterate

    Unblock Art, Dev and QC, ship on time, then iterate with live data and UA tests.

Context & goal

Match puzzles are one of the biggest casual genres, but music in them is almost always passive background. The goal was to bring Amanotes' music strength into this genre: a 3D match and color-sort puzzle where every blend fills an artwork with color and music, so the music becomes the reason to keep playing.

Challenges

  • ConceptNo proof yet that players would notice or value music in a match puzzle. Building the full game first would have been a costly bet.
  • DesignMusic played behind the gameplay without reacting to it, so it added little to the experience.
  • ProductionEvery level was built and checked by hand against its artwork. Content became the bottleneck for the first release.

What I changed

  • Validated the music-led idea with AI-assisted playable prototypes before committing the full team. AI prototypingConcept testing
  • Made each match, blend and color fill unlock music and ASMR sounds, turning music into feedback for every action. Core loop & USPGame design
  • Co-built an AI-assisted level tool with the GD team. It generates levels from artwork and previews each one, so designers check it against the intended level design before export. AI in workflowLevel design
  • Planned scope by milestone and ran weekly sprints, stand-ups and release retros with the team. Sprint planningScope & risk
  • Ran playtests to measure how the music design changed engagement, then fed results into the next sprints. PlaytestingKPI analysis
  • After launch, shipped regular updates: a new onboarding flow (FTUE), new levels, fruits and artworks, and UI/UX refreshes. Live operationsRelease management

Results

+15% D0 play time from the music design variant-25% first-release production time300+ levels generated & previewed with multiple difficulties

Context & goal

A revamp of Rhythm Duet gameplay. Players drag a 3-cat band to catch notes, and each cat plays its own instrument. The goal was a more personal music experience, where players decide how a song sounds.

Challenges

  • DesignPlayers couldn't change how a song sounded, so every playthrough felt the same and there was little reason to replay.
  • ProductionSeveral characters, instruments and maps meant a large volume of art for the team to produce.
  • TechMusic had to follow each player's choice. Every instrument needs its own track for every song, and the game has many songs, so pre-producing every combination wasn't realistic.

What I changed

  • Added a cat recruitment system: each cat brings a different instrument, so players can remix any song, such as a piano song played on violin and guitar. Feature designCore loop & USP
  • Worked with the team on a solution that swaps music layers in real time, so the song plays with the instrument sounds the player picked. Technical problem solvingMusic & sound direction
  • Worked with the Art team to generate characters, instruments and maps with AI. AI in workflowCross-team alignment
  • Ran production in weekly sprints from revamp concept to build. Sprint planningProduction

Results

-25% first-release production timeReal-time instrument mixing shippedAI-made characters, instruments & maps

Context & goal

A newly formed studio with a promising Idle Simulation × Music concept but no experience shipping and testing a music game. I onboarded them from scratch. The goal was a first release on a testing track, then passing the concept, gameplay and core-loop tests to earn the next product phase.

Challenges

  • ConceptMusic hid the USP. The background song didn't fit the theme. In a music game players need to recognize the song to engage, so the game's main selling point wasn't landing.
  • ProductionScope creep. The team kept adding features instead of finishing a playable build, putting the first release date at risk.
  • TestingUnreliable test data. Errors in SDK, ad mediation, Firebase and AppsFlyer setup left test data missing.
  • DecisionUnclear phase decisions. The team was unsure whether to continue or pivot, and what each test phase should deliver.

What I changed

  • Suggested a new song list that fits the theme, as the first songs players hear when they open the game. Player insightUSP design
  • Cut scope to a core-loop build and set phase-based checkpoints to track progress against deliverables. Scope & timelineMilestones
  • Introduced an integration checklist plus a QC pass before each test. ProcessQuality
  • Ran a scorecard review with go / iterate / stop criteria and defined outputs for each phase. Decision makingPlaytest analysis

Results

Players stayed noticeably longer with the new song listFirst release in 6 weeksClean data in every test after QCPassed all 3 test phasesIAP & IAA monetization validated

Context & goal

A newly formed studio building 2 Sort Puzzle × Music games. I onboarded the team, evaluated both game ideas, and set the goal: a first release on a testing track for each game, then a gameplay test that measures the impact of the music.

Challenges

  • ReleaseStore policy risk. Parts of the UI/UX didn't meet Google Play policy, blocking the production release.
  • TestingUnreliable test data. Errors in SDK, ad mediation, Firebase and AppsFlyer setup left test data missing.
  • DecisionUnclear phase decisions. The team was unsure whether to continue or pivot, and what each test phase should deliver.

What I changed

  • Wrote a song plan per level, plus logic for how each stem (drums, bass, melody) reacts to player actions. Game designMusic direction
  • Specified UI/UX changes to meet Google Play policy before submission. UI/UXStore management
  • Introduced an integration checklist plus a QC pass before each test. ProcessQuality
